Content and learning expert.

I write about what actually holds up when you test it: whether AI-generated content is precise enough to trust, whether a quality claim survives independent scrutiny, and whether the learning science everyone cites actually works at scale, not just in a lab.

I'm VP of Content at Save My Exams, where I build and measure the quality of exam-revision content used by students across GCSE, A Level, IB, AP, and more. My work sits at the intersection of two things rarely done well together: rigorous, empirically-tested quality measurement, and genuine learning-science depth — not citing the research, but running it, at scale, on real data.

Writing

Accurate is not the same as right

AI can be accurate and still fail a student: wrong exam board, or a summary that skips the retrieval work revision depends on. Precision, not correctness, is the real product.

The work behind a quality claim

Most AI trust claims are a rubric with no evidence anyone agrees on it. I tested mine on six reviewers before trusting it. One criterion, tone, failed completely.

Speaking

I speak on the intersection of content quality, AI, and learning science — grounded in real data and real methodology, not vendor claims. Topics I'm currently developing:

  • Why "AI-generated" and "accurate" aren't the same claim — what gets lost when content is technically correct but not specific enough to the exact context a learner needs.
  • Measuring quality honestly — what it actually takes to test whether a quality framework produces agreement between independent reviewers, and why most published "quality" claims never check.
  • What learning science looks like at platform scale — testing decades-old lab findings against millions of real, self-directed users, including the results that don't cooperate.
